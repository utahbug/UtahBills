## Bounded collection and retention

Each enabled source gets one robots request and one RSS request per manual run. HTTPS and the configured hostname are enforced before following redirects (maximum three); 15-second socket timeout; four MiB response cap; robots capped at 128 KiB; at most 200 RSS items. RSS/metadata schema checks reject HTML challenge pages, empty results, future/invalid dates, invalid links, conflicting duplicates, DTDs/entities and null-byte XML encodings. Atom is not implemented because the investigated sources are RSS. No immediate retries on rate limits/errors. A failed enabled source produces exit status 2 after preserving metadata and its health report; disabled outlets are not reported as successful acquisitions.

ETag and Last-Modified support conditional requests. A 304 requires a previously accepted baseline. Successful checks advance that source's last-success timestamp; failures record a separate last-attempt/error without advancing success or deleting retained records. Each source validates its complete update before applying it. One failure does not prevent another enabled source succeeding. State writes use a temporary file, flush/fsync and atomic replacement. Corrupt saved JSON/schema stops rather than resets history. No feed body is saved.

Stored metadata: outlet/source, URL, GUID, headline, byline if available, original publication value/date/timezone precision, observed revision hashes, first/last seen and source health. Publication time is not retrieval time. Missing timezone stays unknown. Metadata changes append revisions; body-only corrections cannot be detected without an approved separate review method and must not be claimed detected. Unknown author remains unknown and would block attribution-complete publication approval.

Deduplicate by outlet plus canonical URL/GUID, stripping only known tracking parameters/fragments. Preserve meaningful query parameters. A stable GUID can carry a new canonical URL under the same identity, retaining old revisions. Conflicting retained GUID/URL identities reject that source update. Cross-outlet/syndicated copies are not silently merged. Articles missing from a later feed are retained, including prior editorial decisions. Retention/purge policy remains an owner decision; no automatic deletion is enabled.

## Research matching and five proposal cases

`queue` is offline. It verifies checksums of the saved official 2027 request and introduced indexes, exact session/bill metadata identities, and historical roster evidence. Only direct stable-ID links in the 2026 roster supply name cues; unresolved IDs and inferred aliases are omitted. Historical names are search clues, not claims of 2027 membership or affiliation. Current contacts are not used as successor evidence.

Headline title phrases, literal reported sponsor phrases and shared subject words produce candidates with explicit reasons, never verified associations. Headlines alone have low recall and false positives; this is not a comprehensive legislative-news classification system. A named legislator is not automatically a reported sponsor: separate people mentions from explicit sponsors/introduces wording. These literal cues remain unverified and incomplete, not official sponsorship facts. Missing sponsor names are not filled from related requests. Candidate records retain exact session-qualified IDs and source provenance. Raw request-feed entries may include source-observed requests without verified status; the research tool does not promote their qualification. Matching uses current saved titles only, so historical-title changes may be missed. Multiple potential records/proposals remain separate. Same-title different-session proposals cannot silently collapse into an identity.

The queue separately retains manual case packets for municipality repeal (versioned draft), Albrecht's nuclear regulatory draft, Owens' campus policy discussion, data-center/MIDA policy slides and digital-plate agenda/news discussion. Two nuclear proposals remain separate despite one article. These packets are not promoted into the saved catalog or treated as proof of request-to-draft identity. Exact evidence boundaries are in the Phase 1 inventory; no new verified associations were established.

## Simple editorial workflow

1. Run `python scripts/news_research.py collect` manually when an approved metadata check is wanted. This command is independent of every official refresh script.
2. Run `python scripts/news_research.py queue`; inspect `test-results/news-research/queue.json`, its source dates/limitations, candidate reasons and the five manual case packets. Visit the original reporting and exact official records using permitted access.
3. Decide on each distinct proposal, not just the article. `review` accepts `--article-id`, `--revision-hash`, `--proposal`, `--decision accept|reject|defer`, `--reviewer`, `--reason`, optional repeatable `--official-evidence`, and `--kind association|description|background`. A description additionally requires manually supplied `--proposed-text` (up to 2,000 characters); the program never generates one.
4. Acceptance requires official evidence links and the current metadata revision. It means accepted **for research review**, not identity verification or publication. Reviewer names are self-declared local audit fields, not authenticated proof of independent approval. Evidence URLs alone are not validated proof of their contents. Every decision keeps `verification: unverified` and `publishable: false`.
5. Retain decisions append-only. Changed metadata revisions exclude earlier approvals from the current queue and require re-review. Rejections and deferrals retain reasons; one article can have separate decisions for multiple proposals. Never silently overwrite an earlier decision.
6. Before a future verified/public state, independently establish both article-to-proposal and proposal-to-exact-record links; pin official session, ID, draft/version, source bytes/hash and relevant passages; review exceptions and conflicts. A second designated editor and explicit publication authorization remain necessary. Title/sponsor similarity or an `accept` command cannot satisfy that gate. Keep attributed reporting/background separate from official statutory descriptions.
